What is a Bond?

A bond is a debt security issued by a company that offers the bond holder a fixed annual yield and principal repayment at maturity.

When you buy a bond, you are essentially lending a company money, and in repayment they are offering you annual interest payments.

Why buy bonds?

Bonds are a great way to diversify your investment portfolio and reduce risk. They can provide a steady stream of income and are generally considered to be less volatile than stocks.

Additionally, bonds can be a good option for investors looking for a more conservative investment strategy, as they tend to be less affected by market fluctuations.

How to buy bonds

You can buy bonds through a broker or directly from the issuing company. When purchasing bonds, it is important to consider the credit rating of the issuer, as well as the interest rate and maturity date of the bond.

It is also important to understand the risks associated with investing in bonds, such as interest rate risk, credit risk, and default risk.

Let's give an example with easy numbers:

  1. Say you purchase a bond for $100 on January 1st, 2025 from Google
  2. The bond has these components:
    • The face value, or principal, is $100
    • The coupon rate is 5%, which means you will receive $5 in interest payments each year
    • The maturity date is January 1st, 2030, which means you will receive your $100 principal back on that date
  3. On January 1st, 2026, you will receive your first interest payment of $5
  4. On January 1st, 2027, you will receive your second, and so on...
  5. Upon maturity, you will receive your $100 principal back
  6. So, your total investment on January 1st, 2030, will be comprised of $25 in interest payments and your original $100 principal back, for a total of $125
