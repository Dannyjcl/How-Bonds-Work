More About Bonds

This is the second page with more detailed information about bonds.

Who Else Issues Bonds?

Bonds are issued by various entities, including governments, municipalities, and corporations.

These bonds are classified based on who is borrowing the money from the investor.

Government bonds are issued by national governments and are considered low-risk investments.

Municipal bonds are issued by local governments and can offer tax advantages to investors.

Corporate bonds are issued by companies and typically offer higher yields but come with higher risk.

What is Interest Rate Risk?

Interest rate risk is the risk that bond prices will decline as interest rates rise.

This is referred to as the "seesaw rule" because as interest rates rise, the value of your bond principal decreases, and vice versa. This is because new bonds are issued at the higher interest rates, making your lower-yielding bond less attractive to investors.

For example, if you purchase a bond with a 5% interest rate and interest rates rise to 6%, the value of your bond will decrease because investors can now purchase new bonds with a higher yield.

What is Credit Risk?

Credit risk is the risk that the bond issuer will default on their debt obligations and fail to make interest payments or repay the principal.

This risk is higher for corporate bonds, especially those issued by companies with lower credit ratings.

Investors can mitigate credit risk by diversifying their bond portfolio and investing in bonds with higher credit ratings.
